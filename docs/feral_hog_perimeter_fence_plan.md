# Comprehensive Feral Hog Perimeter Fence Plan
**Project Scale:** 50-Acre Perimeter (Approx. 5,940 Linear Feet / 1.1 Miles)
**System Type:** High-Tensile Fixed-Knot Woven Wire Fence with Tractor-Assisted Installation

---

## 1. Project Overview & Cost Summary
Building a secure perimeter over 50 acres requires a high-tensile system that resists intense rooting and animal impact without sagging over time. This plan leverages a tractor with a 3-point hitch to mechanize clearing, demolition, and tensioning.

### Estimated Cost Matrix
* **DIY Material Cost:** $10,500 – $15,200 (Assumes square perimeter, standard terrain)
* **Contractor Rate (Turnkey):** $39,100 – $61,900+ (Includes commercial clearing and demolition labor)

---

## 2. Core Material Specifications

* **Fence Fabric:** 35" to 42" High-Tensile Fixed-Knot Woven Wire (e.g., Bekaert Solidlock Pro). Standard 12.5-gauge wire with Class 3 Galvanization or zinc-aluminum coating. Vertical stays must be spaced 3" to 6" apart with a graduated horizontal mesh (tighter at the bottom).
* **Line Posts:** 6.5 ft Heavy Steel T-Posts (1.33 lb/ft grade) spaced exactly **20 feet apart**.
* **Brace/Corner Posts:** 6" to 8" diameter, 8 ft pressure-treated wood posts. Driven or buried 36 inches deep with concrete backfill.
* **Fasteners:** Gripple wire joiners, heavy-duty T-post clips, and 9-gauge brace wire.

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Execution Plan

### Phase 1: Demolition & Scrap Removal (Tractor Assisted)
* **Safety First:** Wear heavy cowhide fencing gloves, safety glasses, and thick canvas clothing. 
* **Clip and Drop:** Cut old wire clips from existing T-posts using fencing pliers. Deposit all debris immediately into buckets to prevent "hardware disease" in livestock.
* **Mechanized Spooling:** Hook a 3-point hitch wire-winder or hydraulic spooler to the tractor. Attach the loose end of the old barbed wire and engage the spooler to safely draw and coil old strands out of the overgrown brush.
* **Post Extraction:** Wrap a heavy log chain around the base of the old wood/steel posts. Attach to the tractor's 3-point drawbar and lift straight up using the rear hydraulics to salvage posts cleanly.

### Phase 2: Corridor Clearing
* **Width:** Clear a minimum **10-foot wide corridor** down to bare dirt around the entire 1.1-mile line.
* **Debris Management:** Stack or mulch cleared trees and brush at least 15 feet *inside* the property line. Do not stack debris on the outside, as it creates an accidental ramp for hogs to climb over the fence.

### Phase 3: Structural Framing & Layout
* **H-Braces:** Install heavy wood H-braces at all 4 corners and gate openings. Install intermediate inline H-braces every 1,320 feet (1/4 mile) on straight runs to anchor the extreme horizontal tension.
* **Post Driving:** Use a 3-point hitch hydraulic post driver to smash T-posts into undisturbed ground. Face T-post studs *outward* (toward the hog side) so impact forces push the mesh into the post rather than shearing clips.

### Phase 4: Wire Unrolling & Tensioning
* **Unrolling:** Load 330-foot fixed-knot wire rolls onto a 3-point wire dispenser and drive down the cleared corridor to lay the mesh flat.
* **Stretching:** Clamp a heavy-duty steel pull bar vertically across the wire fabric. Connect the pull bar to the tractor drawbar using chains and slowly drive forward to stretch the 330-foot run taut before securing it to the posts.

## 4. Engineering Solutions for Problem Areas

### 🚚 Drive-Through Truck Gates (Qty: 2)
* **Gate Selection:** 14-ft to 16-ft heavy-duty, 6-bar galvanized tube gates.
* **Mesh Skirt:** Wire-tie leftover fixed-knot panel mesh along the bottom half of the gates, hanging low enough to nearly brush the ground.
* **Concrete Threshold:** Pour a 6" wide by 8" deep concrete grade beam sill flush with the dirt between the gate posts. This solid barrier stops hogs from rooting a trench beneath the tire track.

### 🕳️ Low Areas & Dips (Qty: 4)
* **Anchoring:** Position a heavy wood or thick steel post at the lowest point of each depression.
* **Earth Anchors:** Use screw-in mobile home earth anchors or pour a small concrete footer to tie the bottom wire down. This stops the fence's high tension from pulling the posts out of the ground.

### 🌊 Creek Crossings / Water Gaps (Qty: 2)
* **The Design:** Swing-Panel Floodgate system.
* **Structure:** Set two heavy utility poles high up on opposing banks above the flood line. Run a thick steel cable or oil-field pipe horizontally between them.
* **The Flaps:** Cut standard 4-gauge hog panels into 3-foot wide sections and hang them from the cable using heavy steel rings or chains. 
* **Function:** During low water, gravity keeps the heavy panels vertical to block hogs. During floods, water and debris push the panels open to prevent a dam effect. The panels drop back into place as water recedes.

## 5. Ground-Level Anti-Dig Add-Ons (Pick One)
1. **Solar Electric Offset:** Run a smooth 12.5-gauge high-tensile wire 6 inches off the ground and 6 inches outward from the fence mesh using T-post extension insulators. Power it with a 2-to-3-Joule solar energizer to deter snout-digging.
2. **Integrated Mesh Apron:** Opt for fixed-knot rolls featuring an extra 12-to-18-inch ground skirt that pins flat on the dirt facing outward.
