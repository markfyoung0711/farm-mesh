# Wagon Running Gear: Parts List and Build Notes

Planned use: hauling brush bundles, rolls of wound wire pig panel, T-posts, and tools. Light-duty, bulky loads, mostly on private property.

> Weights and sizes below are rough estimates. Verify against your actual materials, axle ratings, and a published beam-capacity chart or a welder before building.

---

## 1. Parts List

### Front end (steering)
- **Front bolster**: steel beam the deck rides on
- **Fifth wheel or turntable**: plate or ring that lets the front axle pivot under the bolster
- **Kingbolt (kingpin)**: pivot pin through the center
- **Tongue or hounds**: with a clevis or drawbar hitch on the end
- **Hitch pin and safety chains**

### Rear end
- **Rear bolster**: fixed beam on the rear axle for the deck to sit on
- **Reach (coupling pole)**: long member connecting front and rear axles, usually with a series of holes to adjust wheelbase
- **Reach clamp or bolts** at each axle

### Axle components (if not included with your axles)
- Spindles, hubs, and bearings
- Seals and grease caps
- Wheels and tires, matching bolt pattern and load rating
- Brakes (surge or electric) if towing at road speed or hauling heavy loads

### Hardware and extras
- Grade 8 bolts
- U-bolts and spring plates or clamps for axle-to-bolster mounting
- Stake pockets or bunk mounts on the bolsters
- Lights and reflectors
- SMV (slow-moving vehicle) triangle if used on public roads

## 2. Estimated Load Weights

| Item | Rough weight |
|---|---|
| Brush bundles | A few hundred lb each; wet green wood is much heavier |
| Roll of pig panel | A few hundred lb, depending on panels wound together |
| T-posts (6-7 ft) | About 8-10 lb each; bundle of 25 is around 250 lb |
| Tools | Negligible |

A full, heavy day is probably **1,500-3,000 lb**. Axles rated **3,500 lb each or more** give a comfortable margin. Heavy-equipment parts are not needed.

---

## 3. Design Recommendations

- **Deck instead of a closed box**: A flat deck with removable stakes or a rack on the bolsters lets you load long brush and roll panels on from the side.
- **Bolster width**: 6-8 ft gives a stable deck for rolls and bundles. Wider carries more brush per trip but limits you in gates and on trails.
- **Bolster material**: Steel tube such as 3x3 or 4x4 at 3/16" wall is a common choice and generally ample for this load. Confirm with a beam-capacity chart or a welder.
- **Reach length**: A longer reach helps with overhanging brush but flexes more. Keep it as short as your load allows, and use heavier tube or a trussed design if you go long.
- **Brakes**: Probably unnecessary for low-speed dragging on your own property. Add them if you tow with a truck at road speed or on hills.
- **Tires**: Choose a load rating comfortably above the axle rating. Ag or trailer-rated tires are better for rough ground.

## 5. Hauling an Existing Running Gear (Disassembled)

If you are moving a used gear in a truck with an 8 ft bed:

- **Weight**: Likely around 1,500-2,500 lb total depending on tires and options (verify).
- **Remove wheels and tires first.** They are a big share of the weight, and each can be 100+ lb.
- **Split into two trips** if you are near your truck's payload rating (see the door-jamb sticker).
- **Reach**: This is the part that will not fit in the bed. Collapse or remove it. If it hangs past the tailgate, tie a red flag to the end (most states require one).
- **Loading**: Use a tractor loader, forklift, or engine hoist. The front axle and bolster assembly is the heaviest single piece.
- **Securing**: Strap heavy parts low and against the front of the bed with ratchet straps rated for the load, not bungees.
